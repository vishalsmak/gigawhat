## 1 Purpose

This procedure sets out how lone work is planned and monitored, what happens when a lone worker misses a check-in, and which tasks must never be done alone.

## 2 Scope

All employees and contractors who work alone for any part of a shift: at substations, governors and pressure reduction stations, on the networks, in customers' premises, in depots out of hours, or while driving for work.

## 3 References

## 4 Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lone worker | Anyone working where no colleague can see or hear them and raise the alarm. |
| Monitoring controller | The Control Engineer for electricity staff, the Gas Network Controller for gas staff. Corporate staff use the controller for the site they visit. |
| Lone worker device | A company device with an emergency button, man-down alarm and location tracking, linked to the monitoring controller. |
| No-signal site | A site on a business unit's no-signal list, where mobile phones do not work reliably. |
| Out of hours | 18:00 to 07:00 on weekdays, and all day at weekends and on public holidays. |
| Duty Manager | The manager on duty or on call for the business unit. |

## 6 Procedure

### 6.1 Tasks that must never be done alone

WARNING: The tasks in 6.1.1 to 6.1.5 must never be done alone, whatever the time or urgency. If a second person is not available, do not start. If a lone task turns into one of these, stop and call for support.

6.1.1 Confined space entry, including the Kingsmead cable tunnel CT-KGM-01 and any governor pit or valve chamber classed as a confined space.

6.1.2 HV switching at 11 kV, 33 kV or 132 kV, including fault switching, and applying or removing circuit main earths.

6.1.3 Work on or near exposed live conductors, testing under a sanction-for-test, and any work under an electrical permit-to-work.

6.1.4 Purging or venting gas, work on gas plant under pressure that may release gas, hot work in a hazardous area, and entry to an excavation deeper than 1.2 m.

6.1.5 Work at height from a ladder above 2 m or from a mobile elevating work platform.

6.1.6 The second person stays for the whole task, close enough to see and hear the worker, and is able to raise the alarm and give first aid.

### 6.2 Before starting lone work

6.2.1 Confirm that the task is not listed in 6.1 and that its risk assessment allows lone working.

6.2.2 Check that your lone worker device and phone are charged and working.

6.2.3 On arrival, check for signal where you will actually work, not only at your vehicle. Where there is no signal, use the site landline, a radio or a satellite-enabled device, or arrange a second person.

6.2.4 Book on with the monitoring controller, giving the site ID or location, the task, your expected finish time and your check-in interval.

### 6.3 Check-in intervals

6.3.1 Check in at least every 60 minutes between 07:00 and 18:00 on weekdays, including routine inspections at operational sites.

6.3.2 Check in at least every 30 minutes for all lone work out of hours and at no-signal sites.

6.3.3 Also check in on arriving at and leaving each site, and book off when lone work ends. A missed book-off counts as a missed check-in.

6.3.4 If you need help or feel threatened, press the emergency button or call 999.

### 6.4 Escalation when a check-in is missed

WARNING: Never assume a missed check-in is a forgotten call. Start escalation at the time the check-in was due.

6.4.1 The monitoring controller calls the worker's phone and device straight away and then every 5 minutes, and tries any site landline or radio.

6.4.2 If there is no contact 15 minutes after the missed check-in, the controller tells the Duty Manager, who sends the nearest available colleague to the last known location. The colleague must not enter a confined space or other hazard to search.

6.4.3 If there is still no contact 45 minutes after the missed check-in, the controller calls 999, gives the last known location and task, and tells the Duty Manager.

6.4.4 If the device raises an emergency or man-down alarm and the worker does not answer within 2 minutes, the controller calls 999 straight away.

6.4.5 Tell the emergency services if the worker may be at a gas escape or near damaged electrical plant. Gas escapes are reported on 0800 111 999 and damaged power lines on 105.

6.4.6 Once contact is made, the controller stands down the escalation, records the times in the lone working log, and reports the event as a near miss under PR-CORP-001.

## 7 Records

Monitoring controllers keep the lone working log for 3 years. Each business unit keeps its no-signal site list and lone worker device test records.
