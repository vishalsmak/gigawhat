## 1 Purpose

This procedure sets out how incidents and near misses are reported, graded and investigated, and how duties under the Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR) are met.

## 2 Scope

All employees, agency workers and contractors, and injury or ill health to anyone arising from our work. Public gas escape reports follow PR-GAS-003 and are reported here only if they meet the gas_escape definition.

## 4 Definitions

Near miss: an unplanned event in which people were exposed to a hazard, or a control failed while they were exposed, but nobody was hurt.

| Category | Use for |
|---|---|
| near_miss | As defined above. |
| gas_escape | An unintended escape that needed an evacuation, ignited, caused a loss of supply, followed damage, or was on an MP or IP main or service. |
| equipment_failure | A failure or defect of plant, protection or a safety device, with nobody exposed. |
| injury | Injury or work-related ill health to anyone. |
| dangerous_occurrence | An event listed as a dangerous occurrence under RIDDOR, such as a fire caused by an electrical short circuit. |
| environmental | A spill or release of oil, fuel or chemicals. |
| security | Unauthorised access, theft, vandalism, lost keys to operational sites, or threats to staff. |

If more than one category fits, injury and dangerous_occurrence take precedence.

| Severity | Criteria | Written report due |
|---|---|---|
| low | First aid or up to 3 days off work; minor defect; spill contained on hard standing; near miss that could only have caused minor injury. | By the end of the next working day |
| medium | 4 to 7 days off work; failure disabling or seriously weakening protection or a safety system; spill reaching soil; break-in, damage or lost keys at an operational site; other gas escapes; near miss that could have caused serious injury or death. | Within 24 hours |
| high | Death, specified injury or over 7 days off work; any RIDDOR-reportable event; gas escape needing evacuation, ignited, or caused by damage to an MP or IP main or service; gas supply lost to 100 or more properties; fire at operational plant; pollution reaching a drain or watercourse. | Within 4 hours |

## 6 Procedure

### 6.1 Immediate actions

WARNING: Never put yourself at risk to make an area safe. Keep people away and call for help.

6.1.1 Stop work, make the area safe if you can do so without risk, and give first aid.

6.1.2 In an emergency call 999. Report gas escapes on 0800 111 999, and power cuts or damaged power lines on 105.

6.1.3 Tell the Control Engineer (electricity) or the Gas Network Controller (gas) straight away. Corporate staff tell their line manager.

6.1.4 After a high severity incident, leave the scene undisturbed except to make it safe or help casualties.

6.1.5 The controller tells the duty manager of any high severity incident at once, and the duty manager tells the Safety Manager within 1 hour.

### 6.2 Making the report

NOTE: Report a near miss even if the timescale has passed. A late report is better than none.

6.2.1 Enter the report in the incident reporting system within the timescale for its severity in section 4. If unsure, use the higher severity.

6.2.2 Record the facts, with site and asset IDs and people by role. Do not record opinions about blame.

6.2.3 The system numbers each report INC-YYYY-NNN. Quote this number in all related records.

6.2.4 The Safety Manager confirms the category and severity within 1 working day.

### 6.3 RIDDOR reporting

NOTE: Only the Head of Health, Safety and Environment, or a named deputy, makes RIDDOR reports for Harrowmere Energy.

6.3.1 The Safety Manager tells the Head of Health, Safety and Environment within 1 hour of a death, specified injury, possible dangerous occurrence or member of the public taken to hospital, and the same day of any injury likely to cause over 7 days off work.

6.3.2 The Head of Health, Safety and Environment decides whether the event is reportable and records why.

6.3.3 Deaths, specified injuries and dangerous occurrences are notified to the HSE without delay, followed by a full report within 10 days.

6.3.4 Injuries causing more than 7 consecutive days off work or normal duties, not counting the day of the incident, are reported within 15 days. More than 3 days off is recorded but not reported.

6.3.5 Occupational diseases, such as hand-arm vibration syndrome, are reported when diagnosed in writing by a doctor and linked to the work.

### 6.4 Investigation and close-out

6.4.1 Complete the investigation within 10 working days for low severity (line manager), 20 for medium (investigator appointed by the Safety Manager) and 30 for high (formal investigation led by the Safety Manager).

6.4.2 Record the root cause, not only the immediate cause, and actions with owners and dates.

6.4.3 If a procedure is wrong or unclear, tell its owner, who quotes the incident number in any revision.

6.4.4 The Safety Manager closes a report only when the root cause is agreed and all actions are complete or moved to tracked work orders.

## 7 Records

Reports, investigations and RIDDOR decisions are kept in the incident reporting system for at least 6 years.
